Before the model, a better question.

A few things to ask before opening a notebook and enthusiastically importing everything.

2 MIN READ

  1. Start with a decision.
  2. Decide what useful means.
  3. Make room for “we don’t know.”

still asking
one more question.

A model can answer a question very precisely and still be unhelpful. Before choosing an algorithm, it is worth working out what someone will actually do with the answer.

Start with a decision.

Imagine a community service wants to understand missed appointments. “Can we predict who won’t attend?” sounds like a modeling problem. “How could we make attending easier?” opens up a different set of possibilities.

The second question invites a look at scheduling, transport, communication, and other barriers. A prediction might be useful, but only if it supports a useful action. A beautifully ranked list does not arrange a bus ride.

Decide what useful means.

Before comparing models, describe the intended decision in plain language. Who will use the result? When will they see it? What could they change? Then consider the cost of being wrong.

  • What happens when the model misses someone who needs support?
  • What happens when it flags someone who doesn’t?
  • Could a simple rule or a clearer process solve the same problem?

Those questions help choose a baseline and a useful measure of performance. They also make it harder to confuse an impressive score with an improvement in someone’s experience.

Make room for “we don’t know.”

The available data may describe yesterday’s process better than tomorrow’s needs. It may also leave out the people who never reached the service in the first place. A model can’t explain an absence it was never given a chance to see.

A good starting brief names the question, the intended action, the available evidence, and the important gaps. It can be short. The point is to make assumptions visible before they acquire decimal places and start looking official.

A useful first sentence: “We need to decide ___, and this analysis could help by ___.” If the blanks are difficult, that’s where the work starts.
